Data-quality findings

  • No NaN coordinates and no non-increasing timestamps in any trip.
  • Time gaps: 1260 gaps over 5 s in total; 23 trips have a gap over 60 s. Largest: trip 90 (153,064 s).
  • Implied speed over 50 m/s at 1531 steps across 42 trips (worst: trip 32, 887 steps), so GPS jumps exist.
  • Trips span the globe (lon -100° to 137°), so we project each trip to metres around its own centroid instead of using degree distances.
  • About 92% of arcs have a reverse twin. Distance alone cannot tell them apart, which is why nearest-segment tops out near 56%.
  • Reference routes are almost fully connected (continuity 100.0%), but 2.5% of train fixes lie more than 50 m from every route arc (detours or off-network driving). We flag these as off-route and keep them out of training and scoring.

Labels and candidates

The .route file lists arcs in order, not one arc per fix. We align fixes to route arcs with a monotonic DP: the route index can only stay or advance, and total point-to-segment distance is minimised. Candidates are up to 8 arcs within 60 m, with 15 features each (distance, rank, direction-aware heading difference, speed, gap, arc length, projection position, node degrees).

HMM / Viterbi, first version

States are the candidate arcs. Emission is the classifier probability. Transition decays with directed hop distance in the road graph, and the hop budget grows with the time gap. Arcs that are unreachable get a fixed penalty, so decoding survives disconnected segments.
